# EP2 "Cold Enough for Company": where the storytelling fails

**Series:** Apocalypses, Connections and Delays
**Episode:** 02, pre-intro plus 18 scenes of 30 s each (about 9.5 minutes)
**Sources:** `EPISODE_2_INTERACTIVE_SCRIPT.html`, `EPISODE_2_PREINTRO_SCENE_00.pdf`, `EPISODE_02_STRUCTURE_AND_NOTES.md`, every EP2 render report and showrunner note from the Pika production session (31 Aug to 4 Sep), and `PRODUCTION_NOTES.md` §61 to §165.

---

## 1. Verdict

The script is very strong. It is a fair-play mystery in which every clue is on screen, in the right order, before the solution. The renders are often beautiful. But a first-time viewer gets very little of it, for four reasons:

1. **The first minute asks no question.** The pre-intro is a safety card read aloud, and its one important beat broke in the render. Scene 01 is a man putting his hand in a wall with no dialogue; the script itself calls it "a man checking a radiator". The question that drives the episode ("He froze in a room that never got cold") only arrives at about 1:30.
2. **Every clue is planted on purpose so the viewer will not notice it, and the 480p renders erased several more.** When the solution comes in Scenes 13 and 14, it plays as someone reading a document and four sentences spoken at a wall. It feels like exposition, not like the payoff of clues the viewer remembers.
3. **The cliffhangers exist only on paper.** Every scene in the interactive script ends with a "Hang the cliff" sentence, but none of them is staged as a shot or a line. Four of the scene-ending buttons that did exist were broken in generation (Sc00, Sc06, Sc08, Sc12).
4. **Nobody on screen is in danger until Scene 16.** Kell is already dead when the episode starts, so for 15 scenes the danger is in the past. The climax line, Tomas's *"I would prefer you did not die"*, answers a threat to Beatrice that the audience has never seen.

All four are fixable with **6 replacement scenes, 6 short insert shots and a list of free edit changes** (§6 and §7). Everything else in the cut can stay.

## 3. Where the storytelling fails

### 3.1 The first 60 seconds ask no question

| Time | What the viewer sees | What the viewer wonders |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:30 | A paper creature reads a safety card to camera. | "What is this show?" |
| 0:30 to 1:00 | A man puts his hand in a wall, looks at it and gets on a train. No dialogue. | Nothing. The script calls this "a man checking a radiator". |
| 1:00 to 1:30 | A frozen man in a warm room, and the case stated. | **"How?"** The first real question, at 1:30. |

On YouTube most drop-off happens in the first 15 to 30 seconds. The episode's best image (a frozen man in a warm room with his tea still steaming) and its best question both come after that window.

**Fix:** a 10-second **cold-open hook** before everything else. It shows the frozen man at breakfast among passengers who don't notice him, and one of them borrows his sugar (insert **C1**). Then cut to the pre-intro, the title, and the card **"06:02 — THAT MORNING"** over Scene 01. Scene 01 now plays as dramatic irony: the viewer knows this man is going to die, so the cold slot is a clue, not a radiator. The 06:02 card also pays off in Scene 09, when the docket reads `BOARDED 06:02`.

### 3.2 The clues are hidden from the viewer, so the solution feels like exposition

The script's rule is that the camera never acknowledges a clue. That rule is right for a rewatch. For a first watch, combined with 480p and AI drift, it means the viewer never consciously holds the clues:

| Clue | Planted in | What survived in the render |
|---|---|---|
| Kell's slot is cold | Sc01 | Visible, but read as a radiator check |
| The PASSENGERS column is empty | Sc03 | **Lost.** The heading drifted out of frame |
| Frost grows outward | Sc04 | Visible, but on a corpse, not a living man |
| The reciprocal register is blank | Sc06 | Visible, but the verdict line was garbled |
| Tomas has no breath | Sc04 / Sc11 | Visible in Sc11 only |
| The ticket was issued 90 minutes early | Sc09 | Visible |

So when the answer arrives in Sc13 and Sc14, the viewer has nothing to put together, and it plays as a lecture.

**Fix: plant the clue visibly, then explain it later.** Let the camera notice each clue for one or two seconds without any character commenting. Then, at the solution, **replay the clues** as one-second flash inserts under Beatrice's lines, the way Columbo or *Knives Out* does it. All of these inserts already exist in your renders except one (§7.3). And close the loop on the Sc03 plant: Amina finally writes a name in the PASSENGERS column (insert **C3**).

### 3.3 The cliffhangers are in the script, not on screen

Each scene's "Hang the cliff" line is narration: a sentence about the scene, not something that happens in it. The scenes actually end on held inserts, cuts on a consonant, or silence. Those endings work in a prestige drama watched as a whole, but each 30-second unit needs to hand the viewer to the next one.

Four of the endings that did exist were broken in generation:

* **Sc00** "I—" merged into the next line.
* **Sc06** "And nobody looked up" became "And nobody else".
* **Sc08** the abort completed, and "It was not intended as a comfort" was cut.
* **Sc12** "Stop" was doubled.

The pattern has the same cause each time, and the production notes already record it: **a short line at the end of a shot, or a break in the middle of a word, gets eaten** (§35, §38, §66, §99, §152). The fix is structural. Put every button on a shot boundary, or give it its own insert.

The act breaks (after Sc04, Sc11 and Sc14) need to be the strongest cliffhangers. At the moment the third act break (Sc14) ends on a procedural line.

### 3.4 Nobody is in present danger, so the climax has no visible motive

Kell is dead before the episode begins. The flicker in Sc11 hints that the route is "looking for the next largest source", and the Sc14 physics note says to establish that. But nothing on screen shows who that next source is. So:

* Sc12 to Sc15 have no ticking clock. The stakes are a train connection in nine hours.
* Sc17's *"I would prefer you did not die"* answers a danger to Beatrice that **the audience has never been shown**. It plays as an abstract confession, when it should be a rescue.

**Fix: give the route's hunger a face, and make the face Beatrice's.** Everything needed is already set up in the script:

* Amina's pods **swing to point at whatever is being drawn from**. This is established in Sc03 ("orienting on him like heads turning") and again in Sc08, so the viewer already knows how to read them.
* Frost **grows outward from the person being drawn from**. This is established in Sc02 and Sc04.
* Beatrice's regard for Tomas is the one genuine, unperformed regard left on the pier. That is exactly why the script has him say the sentence to her.

So:

| Scene | What we add | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|
| **Sc14** end | As Beatrice says "Then I want the name on the consolidation order", Amina's pods swing round together and lock onto **her**. Amina sees it and says nothing. | *The case is solved. The line is still hungry, and the instruments have just turned to her.* |
| **Sc15** end | Insert C2: after "Something outranks me and I can't see it from here", fine rime grows outward across the back of her hand. She doesn't notice. | *She can't see what outranks her. We can see what is already taking her.* |
| **Sc16** end | After she accepts Pikk's refusal, she doesn't ask anyone else. She walks to the wall herself, pushing her sleeve back. There is frost at her throat. | *Pikk said no, so she is going to pay it herself.* |
| **Sc17** start | Her hand is in the slot and the light is dying. Tomas lifts her hand out "like a man closing a drawer" (the same gesture he used on Pikk in Sc08), puts his own in, and says the six words. | The line is now a rescue that the viewer has watched coming for three scenes. |

This doesn't change the solution, the theme or any character's arc. Beatrice still accepts the refusal without argument (the Sc16 beat the script calls "the most important thing she does"). Then she spends herself rather than anyone else, which is the Sc15 signature beat ("I know what I'll be signing") played with her body. Tomas stops her twice: in Sc12, from taking advantage of Ysra, and in Sc17, from paying with her life. And in Sc18, when she asks him *"Did you want to?"*, both of them know she was about to do the same thing.

### 3.5 Exposition scenes have no physical spine

Sc05, 06, 09, 10, 14 and 15 each show four or five people standing still and exchanging 6 to 10 lines in 30 seconds. Your notes on EP1 to EP4 keep returning to this ("too static", "standing like statues", "the feeling of being an observer is below zero"). The EP4 notes already solved it with the **spine** method (§195): one continuous physical action that everyone has a hand in. In the replacements below, each scene has one:

* Sc14: Amina is writing while the others refuse to touch the wall, and the pods turn.
* Sc16: Beatrice walks to the wall.
* Sc17: a hand is taken out of one slot and another goes in.

### 3.6 The comedy is the glue, and it was cut first

The 30-second cap forced cuts, and the cuts fell on the beats that need a pause:

* *"I've done it four hundred times."*
* *"It was not intended as a comfort."*
* The aborted compliment, whose punchline is that the machine interrupts him.

The dark comedy is what makes this show watchable between clues. The Sc08 replacement restores both jokes by putting each one on a shot boundary. The new hook (a passenger borrows the dead man's sugar) adds a laugh in the first 10 seconds.

### 3.7 Viewers can't always tell who is who

Tomas wears a different collar in eight scenes. Ysra had two different faces. Amina was rendered as Ysra once. Beatrice once wore Tomas's panel. Every one of these costs the viewer a moment of "who is that?". The replacements follow the rules that fixed these problems: a plate built image-to-image from the character sheet, Tomas staged large whenever he appears (§143/§147), and each principal given one large front-on shot per scene.

### 3.8 Ysra's arc ends off screen

Her last beat, boarding last with the veil in her hand, is unreadable at 480p in a wide shot of a queue. Insert **P3** gives her a 10-second medium shot. She stands at the carriage step with the veil in her gloved hand, looks back down the pier, and crosses from the cold light into the warm light (the Sc02 colour-temperature rule, used once more).

## 5. Rules the new scene files follow

These are taken from `PRODUCTION_NOTES.md`, and each is written into the prompts:

* Every button line sits on a **shot boundary** or is the protected last sound of the shot (§66, §99, §152).
* Write **where a thing is**, not what is absent (§108/§109). For example: "the slot light stays fully lit; his fingers stay pink", or "his right hand is a fist-shape inside his coat pocket".
* Stage people by **frame side, facing direction and distance from the lens**, never by their relation to each other. Anchor background figures to a frame edge (§23, §94, §115).
* Use **character plates built image-to-image from the sheet** and scope every authority (§122, §134, §154). `ep2_breath_lock.png` is **not** used, because it contains a wrong-collar Tomas.
* **Tomas is large whenever he is on screen**, because the plate only governs the costume at scale (§143/§147).
* When a reference shows a subject in the wrong *state*, it **owns the costume, not the condition** (the Sc04 lesson).
* Unpromptable words are decomposed or avoided. It is always "four hundred thousand joules"; "kilojoules", "Attaché Kell" and "Deputy Registrar" are never used.
* Ambient voices are declared unintelligible (§160).
* No in-video text except one word at macro scale with raking light (§56). Act cards and the time card are drawn in Resolve or Pillow (§190).
